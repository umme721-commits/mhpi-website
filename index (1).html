Maternal Health Policy Institute

Closing the gaps between maternal health policy and the people it's supposed to protect.

MHPI builds tools, research, and strategy for the organizations working on the ground in maternal health — from county-level care access to patient rights at the bedside.

See the work Start a conversation

Ummesalma Palanpurwala founded MHPI to close a gap she kept running into: the people building maternal health policy and the people living its consequences are rarely in the same room.

Her work spans research, legal advocacy, and tools people can actually use — from MaternaMap, a county-level map of where maternal care access breaks down, to the Clinic Kit, a bilingual patient-rights toolkit built for pregnant workers and the clinics that serve them. She's presented this work at national convenings including Drexel Law Review's "Under Pressure" symposium on medical-legal partnerships and maternal health, and the Black Maternal Health Conference. MHPI exists because she kept seeing the same thing: good policy that never reaches the person it was written for.

Nov 2026

Navigating the $70B Productivity Tax

Child care disruptions cost U.S. employers up to $70 billion a year in lost productivity, turnover, and absenteeism — most of it concentrated in "foundational workers," the roughly 80% of the workforce in shift-based, direct-service roles who can't just log in from home when care falls through. That number is doing more work than it looks like. It's one of the clearest proxies we have for how much of the "maternal workforce equity" problem is actually a child care access problem wearing a different name — which changes who should be at the table when employers and policymakers try to fix it.

Source: Moms First & McKinsey, "Foundational Workers Report," April 2026
Nov 2026

Evaluating State-Level Maternal Mental Health Report Cards

Texas earned a C on this year's Maternal Mental Health State Report Cards — exactly at the national average, which itself improved slightly, from a C- to a C. That's the clinical side of the picture: screening rates, postpartum Medicaid coverage, psychiatric consultation access. But 2026 added a new category, Parental Support, scoring paid leave and child care access, and the U.S. earned the equivalent of an F on it nationally. Texas scored 2 out of 5 possible stars — one of 12 states in that band, while 31 states nationwide scored under a single star, and Maine led the country at just 3.5. We've been in direct conversation with the Policy Center for Maternal Mental Health, the organization behind this scorecard, about where MHPI's own state-level work fits alongside it. The gap between the two grades is the real finding: clinical access is improving faster than the structural supports — leave, child care — that determine whether a mother can actually use that access when she needs it.

Source: Policy Center for Maternal Mental Health, "2026 Maternal Mental Health State Report Cards," May 2026
